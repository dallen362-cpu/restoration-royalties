The Science

Why it works — in four moves.

1 · The numbering plan

Area codes + exchanges map to rate centers. Each stadium grid is scored — lot capacity, event depth, local pull, open runway — so a city line is engineered into a market, not picked off a list.

2 · A real local line

The platform sits at the dial-tone layer, so the city’s number is a genuine local line with a branded name on the screen and a listing a guest can find — not an app extension that reads as unknown.

3 · Live capture to the city board

From the first call, every order, catering hold, headcount, lot, and pickup window is captured and written to the city board as it happens — nothing waits for someone to type it up after close.

4 · The city owns its book

The franchisee owns the city address, the line, and the book of guests and caterings. Nothing critical lives on a personal cell phone that walks out the door with whoever was holding it.

Chuck · The Voice on the City Line

Not a chatbot. The voice at the window.

1 · True speech-to-speech

No transcribe-then-reply pause — Chuck hears and answers in under a second. Talk over him and he stops, listens, and adjusts, the way anyone at the window would.

2 · 14 languages, one line

Understands 14 languages and answers in the guest’s own — with real delivery: he can laugh, slow down, and repeat the lot number instead of droning.

3 · The city’s own voice, the city’s own line

A voice matched to the brand, riding on the city’s own Smart Number. The number stays with the city; the intelligence rides on it — never a rented extension somebody else controls.

4 · He acts while he talks

Mid-call Chuck does the work: reads the menu, checks the lot and the date, holds the catering before the hangup — then writes the headcount, the window, and the notes to the city board.

Questions Operators Ask

Straight answers.

Is this a franchise offer?
No. Half a Cow is a concept preview — an illustration of what a national food-truck franchise on this platform could look like. Nothing on this page is an offer to sell a franchise. A franchise can be offered or sold only through a registered Franchise Disclosure Document (FDD), and only where registration and applicable state requirements are satisfied.
What does the $25,000 figure actually mean?
It is an illustrative SLA structure for discussion only — the shape of what a city package could contain: the city URL and private-label storefront, the stadium rate-center Smart-Number territory, the gold-standard city site, and the operating platform. It is not a price, not a quote, and not an offer, and it may never exist in that form.
Are the phone numbers on this page real?
They are illustrative examples. Every vanity, grid, and area code shown is an example of what a franchisee could operate — not owned, not reserved, and not provisioned. Availability is confirmed only at activation. The area codes themselves are public numbering-plan facts; the numbers built on them here are placeholders.
What is the NFL connection?
None, in the legal sense. The territory concept simply uses the 30 pro-football markets as a map, because game-day, tailgate, and event demand is what a beef truck is built for. Team, city, and stadium names appear in text only, for reference — no logos or marks are used, and this concept is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or sponsored by the NFL or any club.
Do I need restaurant experience?
The concept is built so an operator can run a city without a back office: the master carries the brand, the menu spec, the sourcing standard, the food-safety checklist, and the guest script, and the platform answers the line, holds the catering, and routes the truck. What the operator brings is the work ethic and the game day. Describes the concept’s structure; no outcome is promised.
What would an operator actually be buying?
In the illustrative structure: a city — the address, the stadium grid, the storefront, the standard, and the platform behind them — instead of a truck and a hope. What that would cost, what it would include, and what any operator could expect from it would be defined solely by a registered FDD. No revenue, income, or profit is represented or implied (FTC Franchise Rule, Item 19).
